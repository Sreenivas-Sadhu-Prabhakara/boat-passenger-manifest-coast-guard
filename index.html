How it works

One voyage, one manifest. You name the vessel, the master, where you are leaving from and going to, and when; then you list every person boarding. The tool adds up the passengers, applies whatever child rule you have set, adds the crew, and compares that total with the authorised passenger capacity you typed in from the vessel's own certificate. In plain words:

counted = passengers (after your child rule) + crew and the manifest is issued only if counted ≤ authorised capacity.

Six things stop a manifest being issued, and they are refusals, not warnings. No passengers listed. An authorised capacity left blank or zero — the figure is never guessed for you. A total counted over the capacity, in which case the tool names how many persons must come off. A head count written on the manifest that does not equal the names listed. The same passenger entered twice. A required particular missing from any row. Printing stays locked until each one is cleared, because an over-capacity banca is the single most common way people die in this trade.

Closing the voyage is a second step. Until you record the arrival time and the head count on arrival, the voyage shows as open and stays open in the log. An arrival earlier than the departure is an error, and a head count on arrival that does not match the number who departed is an error that names how many persons are unaccounted for. That reconciliation is the entire reason a manifest exists.

Everything is held in this browser on this device, so it works at the dock with no signal. Nothing is uploaded.

What the authority sets, and what we set

Passenger-manifest requirements in the Philippines come from the Philippine Coast Guard and the Maritime Industry Authority (MARINA), and they differ by vessel and by route. We ship no table of capacities, no copy of any official form and no schedule of fees or fines. Three things we can point at a document for, and we name it: that the persons allowed aboard are limited to the authorised passenger carrying capacity including the crew; that the manifest indicates the name, age and gender of the passengers listed; and that it goes to the nearest Coast Guard unit, with the Master's Oath of Safe Departure, at least one hour before scheduled departure. All three are from DOTC Department Order No. 2000-25 (18 April 2000), which is old enough that you should confirm it has not been amended for your route.

Everything else in the list below we could not source, so we do not assert it: we give a starting figure, label it indicative, and let you change it. In particular we do not know your vessel's authorised capacity, whether children count differently toward it, or how many life vests of which sizes your authority requires. Check MARINA's memorandum circulars and the Coast Guard for what is in force, or ask the officer who inspects you.

Assumptions & constants

Every figure this tool uses is declared once in assets/manifest.js and printed below. Three are sourced to Department Order 2000-25: crew count against the authorised capacity; name, age and sex are the named particulars; one hour is the lead time for handing the manifest in. The rest are our starting figures, and all of them are yours to override — the authorised capacity has no default at all, by design.

ConstantValueStatus
This tool records and prints. It transmits nothing and files nothing: it does not submit a manifest to the Coast Guard, to MARINA, to a port authority or to anyone else, and it has no network access of any kind. Handing the manifest in is still your job, by whatever means your boarding officer requires. The printed sheet is a complete record of the voyage, not a reproduction of any prescribed form — we do not reproduce official forms because we cannot verify which version is current, so copy the particulars onto the form you are given if you are given one. It is not legal advice, it does not clear you to sail, and it is no substitute for the inspection, the certificates or the master's judgement. Where this tool and the document in your hand disagree, the document wins.
